## Business economics and expectations already public

Uber matches mobility riders and drivers, delivery consumers/merchants/couriers, and freight participants. More users and use frequency raise trips; bookings translate into net revenue after varying payments, incentives and principal-versus-agent accounting; unit costs, insurance, marketing, staff and platform investment determine profit. Bookings are not revenue. Q2's UK business-model change reduces reported revenue growth without the same change in underlying economics, so simple revenue/bookings 'take-rate deterioration' would be misleading.

Issuer Q2 operating highlights and segment tables disclose bookings $58.022bn, GAAP operating income $1.890bn and non-GAAP operating income $2.143bn. Delivery and mobility contribute profit while freight remains a small loss and central costs consume part of segment profits. Growth, engagement and leverage are favourable evidence; they do not independently prove customer switching costs or lasting market power. Paid incentives, driver availability, competing apps and autonomous fleets can alter profit per trip. No independent market-share or AV unit-economics forecast is adopted.

The verified public Q3 baseline is **management guidance**, not analyst consensus: bookings $58.25–60.25bn, non-GAAP EPS $0.84–0.88, EBITDA $2.86–2.96bn (5 August release, Outlook lines 47–52). Midpoints $59.25bn and $0.86 are arithmetic, not my forecasts. Official IR, Q2 and event sources searched did not provide a current provider-defined consensus distribution. Search-result claims about other EPS figures were not accepted without date, sample, GAAP/non-GAAP definition and price context. September's Goldman conference is listed on IR, but its full management webcast/transcript was not reviewed. Accordingly this screen cannot assert that August guidance remains the market's latest expectation or that a particular 'beat' would surprise investors.

The interesting conditional question is whether **organic profit per booking and cash available after claims/financing** can improve more persistently than reflected in price. At Q2 volume, a purely assumed ten-basis-point change in non-GAAP operating margin/bookings equals $58.022m quarterly operating income. That is a transparent sensitivity: no probability, persistence, tax rate, future share denominator or valuation multiple is supplied, so it is not converted into an EPS target or stock-price range. The same change can be adverse. A fresh price alone cannot fill the missing expectation and valuation foundations.

## Cash quality, dilution and resilience

The cash-flow statement reconciles Q2 CFO $2.862bn less $70m capital expenditure to FCF $2.792bn; H1 $5.213bn less $135m gives $5.078bn. These are reported operating-cash-minus-capex measures, **not automatically distributable owner cash**. The release's cash-flow SBC addback is $550m Q2 / $1.023bn H1. Subtracting exactly those addbacks as an explicitly crude economic sensitivity gives $2.242bn / $4.055bn; it does not model awards or future dilution, and must not be combined with another deduction for the same future compensation. Source: archived release lines 580–701 and FCF reconciliation 1175–1211.

Important accounting gap: the 10-Q SBC expense note shows $556m/$1.029bn, whereas the release cash-flow addback is $550m/$1.023bn and its EBITDA reconciliation excludes $549m/$1.022bn. Definitions and scopes are preserved separately; the $6m/$1m differences have **not been reconciled**. No convenient explanation is invented. Non-GAAP operating income and non-GAAP EPS retain SBC under the issuer's stated definitions; EBITDA excludes it. It would be wrong to label all adjusted profit 'before SBC'. Archived release Definitions lines 851–910 and reconciliations; SEC browser Note7 lines 926–938.

Accrued insurance liabilities contribute $387m of Q2 CFO and $830m of H1 CFO, approximately 13.5% and 15.9% respectively. This is payment timing, not proof of manipulation or an automatic permanent deduction. Claims ultimately require settlement; reserve development/collateral can change usable liquidity. Insurance liabilities and restricted investments must not be treated as shareholder surplus. Historical diluted weighted shares fall about 3.55% YoY in Q2 (2,125.628m to 2,050.225m), which supports real net per-share improvement despite awards, but does not prove every buyback created value. A historical weighted denominator is not a current fully diluted claim count.

June unrestricted cash/short-term investments total $5.391bn versus carrying debt $12.723bn, a descriptive $7.332bn gap before leases and other claims. It is not a covenant ratio or solvency conclusion. Positive operating cash flow supports holding-window resilience, but pending acquisitions, insurance collateral, legal/tax exposures and refinancing prohibit calling all that liquidity spare. The Q2 filing describes a €14.2bn July bridge commitment for Delivery Hero; a commitment is not money already borrowed. Subsequent financing changes need fresh filing review. The $2.3bn ezCater agreement makes a full forward funding bridge more relevant. No purchase-price commitments are blindly deducted together as if they all settle today.

## Contrary evidence, legal risks and delayed response

GAAP Q2 net income includes $1.6bn pre-tax investment revaluation benefit, so extrapolating $1.17 GAAP EPS as recurring earnings would overstate the operating signal (Q2 release Financial Highlights). Investments also expose equity to reversals. Some legal and transaction costs are excluded from non-GAAP profit despite possible cash consequences. SEC Non-Income Tax Matters describes disputed UK VAT assessments paid while appealing; this is neither a final HMRC victory nor immediately available refund cash. The tax dispute, driver classification and insurance claims can change required cash and unit economics independently of bookings growth.

Independent primary challenge: the FTC case page lists Uber One billing/cancellation allegations, docket 3:25-cv-03477, and a second amended complaint dated 4 May 2026; it labels the case pending. Those are allegations, not established wrongdoing. The page's update date is not verification of every current court-docket event, and no legal outcome probability or loss amount is assigned. Regulator webpage was browser-readable but local download404; rendered excerpt retained. This is case-status evidence, not legal advice.

Hours-to-days delay changes the opportunity: an earnings surprise can reprice before the user reads a notice; a favourable but temporary equity mark may be reversed; slow underlying improvement may require years; clear results can still be followed by multiple compression. Overnight gaps/halts may bypass stop prices, while stop-limits can remain unfilled. A tender deadline or acquisition headline might produce no repricing because already expected; approval delay, expensive financing or integration cost can offset growth. There is no basis for selecting an arbitrary stop or assuming the initial reaction persists for days.

**Reassessment condition:** after the actual Q3 materials, reconcile organic demand versus acquired growth, unit profitability versus insurance/incentives, updated funding and share claims, and management outlook against dated comparable external estimates and the then-executable price. Build adverse, no-repricing, catalyst-delay and correct-but-late cases before defining a ceiling or exit rule. Only evidence of remaining value after the delay and costs could justify promotion to an opportunity dossier; another good quarter alone is insufficient. No alert is issued and no accepted entry/fill is invented.
