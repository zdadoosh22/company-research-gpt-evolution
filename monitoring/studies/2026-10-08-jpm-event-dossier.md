# JPMorgan Chase: October earnings event dossier

**As of 8 October 2026. Decision: reject a pre-event entry; retain JPM as a research candidate.** The scoped event and financial analysis is complete for this rejection, awaiting the parent's independent analytical review. It is not an accepted opportunity, complete company valuation, portfolio allocation or execution instruction. The approved company pilot remains partial.

The potentially favourable mechanism is that deposit balances and lending sustain interest income while credit losses stay contained, causing investors to raise their assessment of repeatable earnings. But much of that improvement is already in July guidance; unusually strong trading and investment gains make the latest headline earnings a poor baseline. The evidence establishes a dated disclosure and substantial financial resources, not an exploitable surprise or a defensible purchase ceiling. This conclusion does not require proving JPM is overvalued.

## Financial resilience through the proposed window

JPM combines consumer banking, commercial/investment banking and payments, and asset/wealth management. Deposit gathering funds loans and securities; client activity generates trading, advisory and fee income. Scale and customer relationships support the favourable case, but neither establishes that elevated trading income or returns persist. Industrial free cash flow and EV/EBITDA are inappropriate decision shortcuts here.

## Guidance, external expectations and what would be a surprise

**Management guidance:** July's outlook was approximately **$105.5bn total FY2026 NII, $96.5bn NII excluding Markets, $107.5bn adjusted expense and 3.2% Card Services NCO rate**; market-dependent where stated ([presentation, slide 8](https://www.jpmorganchase.com/content/dam/jpmc/jpmorgan-chase-and-co/investor-relations/documents/quarterly-earnings/2026/2nd-quarter/9e8f3cef-bd2a-4140-b50f-83c4fba3f23c.pdf)). NII ex-Markets isolates lending/investing/deposit activities more usefully for this question. A Markets NII increase can be offset in noninterest revenue; it cannot automatically become an incremental earnings benefit. Barnum explains the July increase from $95bn to $96.5bn through deposit balances/mix and rates, and cautions about Markets offsets ([transcript, printed p.4](https://www.jpmorganchase.com/content/dam/jpmc/jpmorgan-chase-and-co/investor-relations/documents/quarterly-earnings/2026/2nd-quarter/2Q26-earnings-transcript.pdf)). This is interested management explanation, not independent causal proof.

**Later management commentary:** the [15 September Petno conference transcript](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/jpm/transcripts/737244-barclays-24th-annual-global-financial-services-conference/), Goldberg's quarter-to-date guidance question and following Petno answer, reports expected year-on-year growth in the mid-to-high teens for both investment-banking fees and Markets revenue, subject to disruption. It also anticipates a seasonal Markets decline from Q2 and higher activity-related expenses. This is a Quartr transcript published by Stock Analysis, read in the hash-matched earlier archive and refreshed web page; the original issuer audio was not independently transcribed. It adds relevant public expectations, not independently verified operating results. July alone is therefore an incomplete event baseline. Strong CIB growth has already been publicly discussed, while a sequential trading decline need not be an adverse surprise.

**Calculated hurdle:** H1 NII ex-Markets was $46.957bn; Q2 was $23.677bn ([10-Q, p.19](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/19617/000162828026054343/jpm-20260630.htm)). To reach the July FY guide requires **$49.543bn in H2**, 5.51% above H1, or an average **$24.7715bn per H2 quarter**, 4.62% above Q2. This is an arithmetic half-year requirement, not Q3 consensus or a claim that Q3 and Q4 will be equal. A sequentially stronger Q3 can merely deliver existing guidance. The event must improve the expected future path beyond an already improving baseline to support the proposed mechanism. The September passage does not replace the numerical FY NII guide.

**External estimates, captured 8 October:** [Earnings Whispers](https://www.earningswhispers.com/stocks/JPM), overview/earnings panel, displayed Q3 EPS consensus $5.94, its separate whisper $6.10 and revenue estimate $51.85bn. [TipRanks](https://www.tipranks.com/stocks/jpm/earnings), Earnings Data/history table, displayed $5.90. These are provider-labelled external estimates, not our forecasts and not verified same-basis consensus. Neither opened page supplied an adequate estimate-cutoff timestamp, contributor sample or reconciliation of GAAP/adjusted EPS and managed/reported revenue. The whisper is a distinct proprietary estimate, not an extra consensus observation. Search snippets also lagged the opened pages; the opened values govern this record. Earnings Whispers' displayed 06:40 ET event time conflicts with the issuer's approximately 07:00; the issuer schedule governs.

There is enough external evidence to reject a claim that investors necessarily expect weak earnings, but insufficient basis for a precise surprise threshold. Reading the release/call must resolve the composition of earnings, expense response, reserve changes and forward NII rather than count a mechanical EPS beat. No verified fresh 2027 forecast or independent pre-event estimate of the company's NII/deposit path was established.

At that price, the multiple of **June** tangible book $113.35/share is **2.908×**. Annualizing adjusted Q2 EPS gives $24.56 and a ratio of **13.419×**. Neither is an October forward valuation: book has since moved, and quarterly trading earnings are not a sustainable annual forecast. A **one-off** $1.5bn pretax gain adds about **$0.423 per June period-end common share** before distribution/other equity effects ($1.125bn / 2.658186195bn shares), not a recurring earnings stream. If the same $1.5bn instead raises repeatable annual earnings and the diagnostic 13.419× multiple remains unchanged, the algebraic price increment is **$5.60**; the combined adverse annual-EPS experiment produces **−$8.06** at that same multiple. These are explicitly conditional transmission calculations, **not upside/downside range endpoints or proposed prices**. They assume unchanged valuation and full recognition; a multiple change can dominate them, and neither persistence nor that multiple is independently justified. No accepted price-response range can therefore be built from these experiments.

The reverse-valuation relationship is also conditional: with a steady-state common-equity model, **P/TBV = (r − g)/(k − g)**, so this dated price/book combination implies **r = g + 2.9076(k − g)** for any supplied growth `g` and equity return requirement `k`. `r` here is prospective return on opening tangible equity. Reported ROTCE uses average equity and cannot be substituted. No `k`, `g`, capital-retention path or October book bridge is validated here, so the equation does not produce a unique market-implied earnings forecast. External estimates, guidance and this algebra remain separate.

## Failure modes and conditional reassessment

**No repricing:** good disclosed earnings simply fulfill expectations; extra interest income is offset by expenses/credit costs. There is no evidenced advantage over cash after spread, FX and other actual costs. An EPS beat alone is insufficient to reopen the rejected entry.

**Adverse surprise/gap or halt:** weaker NII ex-Markets, higher funding costs, rising forward credit reserves or constrained distributions can reduce both earnings and the valuation multiple. A pre-event position cannot rely on selling at the prior close or at a stop threshold. A stop-market may execute well below its trigger; a stop-limit may not execute. The reviewed economics do not justify a numerical stop, so the paired price-loss/thesis-exit experiment has no accepted JPM entry.

**Favourable result but delayed reading:** one-hour, four-hour and next-day manual-response delays may leave a fully repriced stock. Each requires newly timestamped bid/ask, FX/cost evidence and re-evaluation against remaining economics; none inherits the pre-event close or assumes a fill. This dossier contains no verified price series capable of proving a delay-tolerant edge. A strategy depending on buying the first surprise is rejected for this manual workflow.

**Event delay:** if the scheduled release does not occur, do not roll the thesis or deadline automatically. Verify the issuer's reschedule and reason; this pre-event research expires at the scheduled disclosure, or earlier on material contradictory news. A delay itself does not create a discount.

**Correct but late:** better franchise earnings might accrue while rates, regulation or market risk suppress the multiple for months. A correct business observation can still lose money within the sleeve's horizon. By 16 October, review whether the call actually supplied a durable, inadequately reflected change. An extension into a months-long hold requires a new valuation/capital basis and dated resolution mechanism; the old event is not sufficient.

Re-review only on new evidence that can change the decision: (1) reconciled forward NII/expense/credit expectations and a material incremental earnings path; (2) Q3 capital, liquidity, deposits and ownership refreshed to the same period; (3) a price materially below a separately supported value of those outcomes after actual spread/FX/fees; and (4) a reaction window compatible with delayed manual execution. Conditions weakening the favourable mechanism are deposit-mix/funding deterioration, higher credit provisions beyond growth, capital use that crowds out distributions, or revenue gains that require comparable compensation/cost increases. No arbitrary percentage trigger substitutes for these links.
