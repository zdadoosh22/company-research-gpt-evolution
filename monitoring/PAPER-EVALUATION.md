# Prospective evaluation questions

8 October 2026. Freeze a cohort protocol before accepting its first entry. Existing fictional test paths are engineering evidence, not this cohort's results. The isolated ledger is under `state/paper`; no broker transaction is implied by an entry or exit there.

Evaluate whether the candidate selection and entry case were supported by evidence available at the time; whether the anticipated event occurred on the assumed scope and timetable; whether plausible adverse outcomes and correct-but-late cases were covered; and whether fees, FX and human delay removed the apparent benefit. Record rejected and missed ideas alongside entries. Continued cash with a recorded reason is an admissible decision.

Compare the frozen price-loss and thesis/time-exit arms on identical entry selections and common comparison deadlines. Record why each exit condition was judged satisfied, quotes actually available after the response delay, nonexecution and partial execution. Retain early-exit proceeds until the common deadline. A stop price is not a guaranteed fill; a stop-limit may remain unsold through a gap.

The first review checkpoint is three completed independently identified event windows, with all rejected/missed candidates retained, plus a cost/FX/delay sensitivity review. Three is an operational experiment checkpoint, not a statistically sufficient sample or a performance promotion rule. Sparse or dependent events remain inconclusive; extend the cohort only through a recorded prospective decision. Do not fit numerical stops repeatedly to these same returns.

Before entry choose cash and an investable passive comparator for the selected security/universe, with matched capital, dates, currency and costs. Missing benchmark prices are explicitly missing, not zero return. Do not retrospectively select a comparator that makes the strategy look favourable. Subscription/electricity costs are separate from broker returns, but relevant to the overall economics.

Report cash plus gross dated marks, estimated liquidation value only where bid/FX/cost inputs support it, realised proceeds, exposure and downside paths, concentration/shared catalysts, turnover, holding time, and missed/delayed execution. No annualisation of a short sample is an accepted performance claim. Missing prices or unresolved actual fill reports prevent an authoritative NAV.

The trial's questions must be answered through actual dated records and independently challenged findings. Positive returns do not cure bad evidence or broken controls; losses do not alone prove the decision process incoherent. Live readiness still requires specific research, policy, data, account, phone and operational acceptance evidence.
