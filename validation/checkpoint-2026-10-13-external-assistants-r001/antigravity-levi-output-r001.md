# Note 2 and continuing-only cash flow: independent challenge (text only)

**Short answer:** No. Note 2 does not give an exact continuing-only operating cash flow (CFO) or recurring owner cash. It gives two things: the gain adjustment used in the indirect method, and the size of the investing proceeds. It does not give the net operating cash of the discontinued business (Dockers).

## (1) The figures, with signs and scope

The gain appears in the income table, and the sale closed on 27 February 2026. So the table cannot be a Q3-only table. I read it as nine months ended 30 August 2026. If it is actually a Q1 or Q2 table, the scope changes, but the identities below still hold.

- **Gain before costs to sell:** +96.3 proceeds − 62.7 carrying amount = **+33.6**. Note 2 says this is the indirect-method adjustment, so it is **−33.6** in the reconciliation from net income to CFO.
- **Recognised gain:** +33.6 − 16.3 direct costs to sell = **+17.3**. This is the same as 96.3 − 16.3 − 62.7.
- **Pretax result:** −23.8 operating loss − 2.9 other expense = −26.7 before the sale. Adding the +17.3 gain gives **−9.4**.
- **After tax:** −9.4 + 0.6 tax benefit = **−8.8** discontinued net income. This is inside consolidated net income.

**What follows from these figures:** the CFO adjustment is −33.6, not −17.3. So the −16.3 costs to sell stay inside net income as an operating charge. Their effect on cash depends on whether they were paid or accrued. Separately, investing cash flows show the full gross proceeds of 96.3.

## (2) Why the tempting shortcuts fail

**Gain vs adjustment vs cash:**
- The recognised gain is +17.3. It is an accounting figure, not cash.
- The adjustment is −33.6. It only reverses the before-costs part of the gain out of CFO.
- The actual cash is +96.3 inflow in investing, plus whatever was actually paid of the 16.3 costs, which sits in CFO through net income and working capital.

**Each shortcut on its own:**
- **Subtracting 96.3 from CFO:** the proceeds were never in CFO. The −33.6 has already taken the gain out, so subtracting 96.3 would remove proceeds a second time. 585.2 − 96.3 has no meaning.
- **Adding 8.8:** −8.8 is an accrual figure. It includes the +17.3 gain, which has already been reversed by more than its own amount (−33.6). It also includes accrued tax and operating items whose cash timing is unknown. Adding 8.8 back would remove a loss that is not the same as Dockers' cash.
- **Adding back 16.3:** this is right only if all 16.3 was paid in cash within the period and recorded as operating. If part was accrued, working-capital changes have already offset it. If it was paid out of the proceeds at closing, the reported 96.3 gross figure would conflict with that. Each case is unknown.

## (3) Algebraic bridge

Consolidated CFO is continuing CFO plus discontinued CFO: 585.2 = CFO_cont + CFO_disc.

Discontinued CFO = (−8.8) + (−33.6) + N + W + T + K, which simplifies to **−42.4 + N + W + T + K**. Here −42.4 = −26.7 − 16.3 + 0.6. The unknowns are:

- **N:** Dockers' non-cash items that Note 2 treats as not significant (depreciation, impairments, share-based pay).
- **W:** Dockers' working-capital changes, including inventory and receivables run-off.
- **T:** the gap between the 0.6 tax benefit and actual cash tax, including any tax on the gain paid by the continuing group.
- **K:** costs to sell that were accrued and not yet paid, which would be added back.

So **CFO_cont = 627.6 − (N + W + T + K)**.

This is not double counting: the gain enters once (+17.3 inside −8.8) and is reversed once (−33.6). The 627.6 is only an algebraic shell. It is not an estimate, because Note 2 does not give W, T or K.

**Recurring owner cash** needs further steps, all still unknown:
- Take continuing CFO and remove the 57.1 principal refund if it sits in operating cash. Its classification is not supplied.
- Remove the 4.7 interest if it is non-recurring.
- Adjust for the timing of transition-service cash, which is not supplied.
- Subtract continuing capex, i.e. 157.9 minus any Dockers capex.
- The 200m share buyback (ASR) is financing and was paid before the period, so it does not belong in this bridge.

## (4) What the "no other significant" wording proves

**It proves:**
- The only significant Dockers non-cash adjustment was the 33.6 gain, so N is plausibly small.
- The only significant Dockers investing cash was the 96.3 proceeds. That supports treating most of the 157.9 capex as continuing, but not exactly.

**It does not prove:**
- Working-capital changes (W). They are not "non-cash items", so the wording does not cover them.
- Cash tax (T) or how the costs to sell were settled (K).
- What threshold counts as "significant".
- That Dockers' operating loss was all cash.
- Dockers' total operating cash flow.
- How the 57.1 refund is classified.

## (5) Evidence request, contrary reading, disposition

**Smallest evidence request:** one figure from the same filing, Levi's 10-Q for the quarter ended 30 August 2026 (Note 2, the liquidity section, or the cash-flow statement): **net cash from operating activities of discontinued operations, nine months**. If that is not disclosed, the fallback is:
- the Q1/Q2 10-Q figure for costs to sell accrued versus paid;
- Dockers' working-capital balances at closing versus the start of the year;
- cash tax on the disposal;
- the line where the 57.1 refund sits.

**Strongest contrary reading:** much of the −26.7 loss and the 16.3 costs may have been accrued, for example wind-down or severance paid in other periods. Dockers' working-capital run-off (W) may also have been a large inflow. If so, Dockers' operating cash could be close to zero or positive. Then continuing CFO would be at or below 585.2, not near 627.6. Note 2 cannot rule this out. Also, if the income table's scope is not nine months, the −8.8 does not line up with the nine-month CFO at all.

**Disposition:**
- **Accept:** the arithmetic identities; that the consolidated CFO, capex and free cash flow figures include Dockers; the bridge with unknowns; and 528.1 / 370.2 only as sensitivities that strip out the principal refund.
- **Withhold:** any exact or point continuing CFO, continuing free cash flow, or recurring owner cash, and any direction for the Dockers adjustment, until that discontinued operating cash figure is obtained.

This is provisional external criticism, not an approval.
