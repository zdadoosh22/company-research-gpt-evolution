Safe claims (with qualifiers)

FactSet's mean Q3 EPS estimate moved from 5.49 (30 June) to 5.93 (9 October cover). That is a pre-event revision within one provider. It is not the event's surprise. The figure is a 14 October retrospective retrieval, so its vintage is unverified.
A managed-revenue context comparison is possible: Hudson's 50,847m (28 September, managed basis, aggregator not disclosed) against issuer managed revenue of about 50.9bn. The gap is about +0.1%, which is smaller than the rounding of "about 50.9" (50.85–50.95bn). So the sign can't be determined at this precision.
Raw EPS gaps can only be stated as conditional arithmetic: 5.57 or 5.75 minus 5.86 gives −0.29 or −0.11, and minus 5.93 gives −0.36 or −0.18. They are not formal surprises.

Agreement between sources says nothing about strategy performance or whether to enter a position.

Missing sample count vs missing EPS mapping: different effects

Missing EPS mapping blocks comparing levels against actuals, because the basis can't be matched. It matters less for a same-provider time series like 5.49→5.93, if the method stayed the same.
Missing sample count doesn't affect basis. It limits reliability, any dispersion-scaled surprise, and checks for changes in analyst composition within a revision series.
Revenue is less affected by either, because the managed basis is stated.
