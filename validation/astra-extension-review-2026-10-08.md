## Minimum analytical machinery

Every candidate needs a short, machine-readable dossier linked to read evidence and a reviewed company record:

1. Exact security, currency/unit, corporate-action basis, event window, anticipated holding deadline and source coverage.
2. Financial resilience through that window, ownership/financing claims and the few business variables capable of changing the thesis. Historical missing values remain missing. Forecast uncertainty may be represented by bounded assumptions.
3. Event-to-business-to-expectations-to-price mechanism. Record management guidance, dated external expectations and model-implied requirements separately. An external consensus subscription is not mandatory; a transparent reverse valuation can be useful, but does not establish what all investors expect.
4. Conditional event outcomes and price ranges, including no repricing, adverse surprise, gap/halts, catalyst delay and a thesis that is right but too late. Explain range construction through economically relevant earnings/cash-flow/multiple/claims effects; a generic percentage target fails.
5. Contrary evidence, reason to prefer the opportunity to cash, uncertainty sensitivities, feasible entry ceiling and frozen exit/reassessment rules. The reviewer must challenge price-response assumptions as well as company accounting.

The existing company methodology's core evidence controls remain valuable. However, completing all three historical pilots, native Excel verification or detailed long-run forecasts irrelevant to the proposed decision should not become universal gates. The addendum must define decision-sufficient scope explicitly and never label a scoped dossier a complete long-term thesis. APP's existing follow-up already supports bounded latest-disclosed snapshots where uncertainty is immaterial; exact unpublished daily cash/shares are not an impossible prerequisite. Conversely, material unsupported margin/renewal economics cannot be cured by adding an uncertainty label.

The first candidate may be rejected after full work. Preserve the dossier and move to the next independently screened candidate; do not force an entry to demonstrate the software.

## Sizing and entry ceilings without invented precision

Willingness to lose £300 establishes an outer capital boundary, not a unique optimal position size, stop, win probability or return hurdle. No dataset can derive personal risk preferences from that statement. The implementer may make a transparent experimental policy choice under the existing delegation, then test its consequences; it must not claim that the number was empirically proven optimal.

A defensible initial policy proposal is three equal **capital-at-risk tranches**, £100 each from the original £300, with at most one new unreported instruction outstanding. Limit issuer or shared-catalyst exposure to one tranche initially; leave unused tranches in cash. This preserves capacity for two later independent experiments if the first position loses all its value. Three is an explicit experiment-design choice, not a measured financial law or assertion that this is the user's required diversification. Compare two- and four-tranche variants prospectively/sensitively rather than pretending three is optimal. Do not replenish a lost tranche through a top-up. Treatment of realised profits should be explicit; a conservative first cohort keeps per-idea cap at £100 and surplus proceeds in sleeve cash until review.

That proposal is implementable now as a paper-policy version. Final adoption should be recorded by the delegated implementer alongside the alternatives. It is not a recommendation to buy £100 of any current watchlist security. Other documented capital budgets are legitimate; leaving all size fields null indefinitely is not necessary.

For an accepted dossier, solve the quantity and ceiling jointly rather than rounding a percentage of NAV:

```
buy_cost(q, p, fx) = q * p * executable_GBP_per_quote_currency
                    + applicable_costs_not_already_in_that_rate
net_exit_s(q)     = q * executable_exit_bid_s * exit_FX_s
                    - exit_costs_s + eligible_net_distributions_s
net_gain_s(q, p)  = net_exit_s(q) - buy_cost(q, p, entry_FX_s)
```

Use major currency units throughout; convert GBX exactly once. Costs can depend on quantity, side and instrument, so solve numerically with monotonic checks rather than assuming one proportional fee formula. Use effective broker conversion rates **or** spot plus a separately computed conversion fee, never both.

Choose the largest broker-permitted rounded-down quantity meeting all of these:

- Total conservative entry cost fits reconciled cash less existing reservations and the chosen tranche cap.
- Stated adverse-event/gap/FX scenarios fit a separately declared scenario loss budget; also disclose full position loss. A stop price never replaces the gap/full-loss scenario.
- Shared-catalyst and aggregate commitments satisfy the policy. Three labels do not create three independent risks.
- The minimum viable broker quantity and uncertainty in costs leave a meaningful potential net benefit. If fixed costs or response uncertainty overwhelm the case, reject it.

For each admissible quantity solve a maximum entry price at which the chosen reference scenario's net proceeds exceed entry cost by the stated required surplus. Build that surplus from cash opportunity cost, explicit model uncertainty allowance and any declared strategy reward requirement. Show the ceiling's sensitivity to each term; do not smuggle in an unexplained 10%, 2:1 reward/risk or annualised target. The uncertainty allowance can be tied to the price impact of plausible perturbations in decisive inputs; it is not a confidence interval unless calibrated as one. Uncertain outcomes still need analytical judgement; scenario arithmetic alone cannot prove an expected edge.

Keep both a share-price limit and a GBP all-in ceiling. A foreign-currency limit controls share price, not GBP spend. At delayed reading, recompute quantity/ceiling if the policy permits a new revision; never tell the user to chase an expired limit. If the acceptable ceiling changes materially under plausible assumptions, report the range and choose a conservative boundary or withhold that candidate.

## Exit policy and delayed execution

Use thesis invalidation and a dated event/deadline as the default analytical exit logic, with explicit portfolio risk controls. Compare an additional price-risk arm on identical paper entries. Do not install a universal percentage stop merely to complete a form.

- **Thesis exit:** freeze identifiable contrary evidence, who can verify it, the maximum tolerable detection/response delay and what to do when evidence is disputed. A missed model job must not leave a previously approved buy instruction current.
- **Time exit:** freeze the catalyst window and final reassessment date; extension requires new evidence and a new revision. Repeatedly moving the deadline after disappointment is a policy change.
- **Profit exit/trim:** reassess remaining prospective reward at the current bid against costs, risks and cash/alternatives. Entry cost is not intrinsic value. A trim can instead be justified by a concentration limit.
- **Price control:** an independently justified level may be an alert/review trigger, a broker stop, or a stop-limit. These are different mechanisms. A manual alert is not a broker-held protective order. Test overnight gaps, halts, wide spreads and nonexecution explicitly.

The SEC order guide explains that market execution price is not guaranteed, limit orders constrain price but may not execute, and a stop becomes a market order when triggered. The manual pipeline must not present a stop threshold as a guaranteed loss cap. [SEC order guide](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/how-stock-markets-work/types-orders), read 8 October 2026.

Paper fill times start after information was available, research completed and the simulated human response delay elapsed. Include next-session and next-day cases, with no assumed availability while the user sleeps. A touched limit is not proof of a fill; intraday high/low bars cannot establish event ordering. Missing executable observations produce uncertain/missed fills, not retrospective best prices. Early-exit proceeds stay in cash to the common comparison date when isolating exit effects. Reinvestment is a separate experiment.

## Costs, FX and financial boundaries

Trading 212 currently documents a 0.15% FX charge for orders in another currency and shows it in the order review; its guidance excludes corporate-event payments such as dividends from that FX fee. This does not remove exchange-rate exposure or other instrument-specific costs. [Trading 212 FX guidance](https://helpcentre.trading212.com/hc/en-us/articles/360018909758-What-is-the-FX-fee-Invest-Stocks-ISA), read 8 October 2026.

The broker documents fractional market, limit, stop and stop-limit orders for most eligible Invest/ISA instruments, pending fractional orders by share quantity, and applicable government/regulatory fees. It also describes execution against reference-exchange bid/offer. “No extra fractional spread” does not mean a stock has no underlying market bid/ask difference. Verify each selected instrument and account preview. [Trading 212 fractional guidance](https://helpcentre.trading212.com/hc/en-us/articles/9511997937437-Can-I-trade-with-fractional-shares), read 8 October 2026.

Required accounting fields: execution and report times, original currency/value, actual effective FX, separately charged costs, GBP debit/credit, broker identifiers, instrument identity, and links to original/revised instruction. Do not manufacture original-currency information for existing GBP-only records. Reconcile broker totals without double counting fees embedded in reported values. Model taxes/levies only where applicable and sourced; an ISA wrapper must not be used as a reason to assume every transaction charge or foreign withholding is zero.

Add corporate actions and historical dividend entitlement or an explicit blocked reconciliation path. Present marked NAV and estimated liquidation NAV separately; use matched timestamps for price/FX. A position can gain in USD and lose in GBP. Existing synthetic results deliberately omit a changing FX path, corporate actions, liquidity and actual broker matching; they cannot validate performance or rank the exit arms.

Report realised/unrealised GBP results, deposits/withdrawals, drawdown, exposure, turnover, trading costs and missed opportunities consistently. Preserve gains within the sleeve; the £300 boundary limits initial external contribution, not a fictitious cap on later NAV. Report incremental operating/subscription costs separately from investment returns. Choose cash and a genuinely investable passive comparator before observing results, with aligned dates/costs/FX and no retrospective benchmark selection.
