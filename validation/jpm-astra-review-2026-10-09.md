## Capital and resilience: challenge both optimistic and adverse readings

The June 10-Q cover confirms NYSE:JPM common stock, distinct from listed preferred depositary shares and notes. Its printed pages 46, 47 and 177-178 support the final capital amounts and applicable holding-company requirements. The archived SEC Note 21 HTML corroborates these; the original PDF tables were freshly extracted and pages 46, 173 and 177 visually checked. The August finalized Standardized CET1 ratio is 14.2%, superseding July's preliminary 14.1%. The exact SLR requirement is 3% plus 1.25%; the table rounds it to 4.3%. No 5% prior-year threshold is carried into June.

Advanced total-capital headroom is $27.66870bn; Standardized CET1 headroom is $57.389780bn. Independently checking additional constraints gives Standardized total headroom $42.85880bn, Standardized Tier 1 $45.50436bn, and a simple holding-company SLR numerator surplus $74.332065bn using the disclosed exposure. This confirms why the smaller Advanced total-capital diagnostic deserves attention. These quantities are overlapping regulatory diagnostics, not additive cash or a complete distribution ceiling. Denominators, instrument eligibility, legal-entity restrictions, stress buffers and management choices can change together.

**Counterchallenge to excessive pessimism:** Advanced total-capital headroom rose from $22.17465bn at December to $27.66870bn at June, a $5.49405bn increase, despite the $78.613bn increase in Advanced RWA. The capital rollforward on page 47 shows earnings, dividends, repurchases and other changes. The dossier's $11.79195bn capital requirement for repeating that RWA growth is correctly conditional on a fixed numerator. It must not be read as a forecast of depletion that ignores retained earnings or eligible capital issuance.

**Counterchallenge to excessive comfort:** average firm LCR declined from 113% in Q2 2025 to 112% in Q1 2026 and 110% in Q2 2026 (page 52). Reported excess eligible HQLA fell to $89.891bn. June uninsured deposits were $1,743.9bn, approximately 64.263% of deposits (pages 54-55), primarily wholesale operating deposits according to management. That mix is not equivalent to assuming every uninsured dollar flees, but it rules out treating the headline liquidity stock as freely available protection. The firm HQLA measure already excludes specified nontransferable bank excess. These observations support the dossier's limited statement that reviewed June evidence does not affirm immediate distress; they do not verify October resilience or a months-long capital/distribution forecast.

The July presentation's printed slide 8 and Barnum's transcript printed page 4 support the $96.5bn FY NII ex-Markets guide and explanation involving deposit balances, mix and rates. The 10-Q page 19 supports H1 $46.957bn and Q2 $23.677bn. Subtraction gives H2 $49.543bn, averaging $24.7715bn per quarter: 5.50717% above H1 and 4.62263% above Q2. Thus sequential improvement can fulfill public guidance. The annual average is not Q3 consensus or evidence of equal Q3/Q4 income.

The favourable mechanism survives scrutiny as a possibility: higher deposit and lending balances can raise income, while fee businesses also improve. However, page 19 shows ex-Markets net yield at 3.65%, down from 3.71% a year earlier, while average ex-Markets interest-earning assets grew. More balance-sheet income can demand more capital and does not establish spread improvement. Barnum explicitly describes offsets between Markets NII and noninterest revenue. Counting a Markets NII increase as an unoffset earnings gain would be wrong; the dossier avoids this.

I read the September archived transcript's complete Goldberg guidance question and Petno response, expense qualification and through-cycle return discussion.
