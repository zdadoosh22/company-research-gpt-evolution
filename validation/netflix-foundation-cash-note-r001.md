# Netflix cash, content and claims foundation r001

Date: 2026-10-09; latest reviewed financial period: June 30, 2026. Scope is a bounded economic reconciliation, not normalized earnings, valuation or trade approval. All currency calculations below are USD millions unless explicitly stated otherwise. Financial statement inputs were USD thousands and divided by 1,000; whole-option/share figures were divided by one million. ## Material new evidence and status

The official SEC June 10-Q MD&A, cash flow discussion (printed p35), identifies $729m of nonroutine cash payments associated with Brazil non-income-tax assessments for prior periods. This is a separate cash foundation from the earlier Q3 2025 approximately $619m operating expense: the reviewed disclosure does not establish that $729m equals that charge plus interest, nor final settlement, complete payment of every exposure, or annual recurrence. Subtracting the termination receipt alone is therefore an incomplete reconciliation of H1 recurring cash. Removing prior-period cash is a sensitivity, not proof cash is sustainably available.

The June 10-Q interest-expense discussion (printed p32) identifies approximately $85m amortization of issuance costs connected with the terminated WBD financing arrangements in Q1. This is an expense-basis adjustment. Do not add $85m to CFO/FCF automatically: amortization is noncash and the operating cash statement already reverses relevant noncash expenses in aggregate. Exact $85m allocation within that reversal is not separately disclosed here.

Annual 2025 content amortization $16,422.166m is authenticated in the annual 10-K cash-flow statement and Note 5: licensed $8,713.558m plus produced $7,708.608m. The issuer Q4 letter independently matches it. This closes the previous missing annual denominator. It does not supply sustainable content-investment assumptions.

These retain both approximation signs and are implied by management guidance, not independently verified sustainable forecasts. A persistent cash/amortization gap, changed title performance or changed amortization life can weaken FCF; no numerical adverse growth probability is invented.

## Termination receipt, taxes and cash reconciliation

H1 CFO $7,034.017m minus cash capex $414.774m equals reported FCF $6,619.243m. H1 net income $8,684.205m includes the gross $2,800m receipt below operating income. H1 tax provision $1,931.467m and an 18% rounded effective rate describe all income, not the receipt's allocated tax and not paid cash. Cash taxes specifically associated with the receipt in H1/FY are not disclosed in the reviewed interim sources. The annual 2025 statutory US21% reconciliation is historical and does not license applying 21% or H1's18% to this receipt.

Define T_H1 as actual cash taxes paid within H1 attributable to that receipt. Gross subtraction alone gives $3,819.243m; removing receipt together with its cash taxes would give $3,819.243m + T_H1. A further sensitivity excluding the $729m prior-tax-period Brazil cash gives $4,548.243m + T_H1. These expressions preserve unknown timing rather than asserting a normalized value. They omit additional possible transactional/timing items and do not assume all cash paid is available for distribution. $271.717m H1 SBC is reversed in CFO; deducting that amount would be a separately labelled economic-cost sensitivity. In valuation choose coherent future compensation-cost versus dilution treatment, with existing claims separately priced; avoid double deduction.

FY reported management FCF guidance ~$12,500m implies H2 reported ~$5,880.757m. Gross receipt subtraction alone gives ~$9,700m before adding back receipt-related cash taxes T_FY.
