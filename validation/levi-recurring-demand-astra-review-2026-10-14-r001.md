## Findings and required corrections

**RD-01 — Medium, nonblocking for the retained park: explicitly carry favorable volume and store evidence.** The gate carefully limits the Americas slight wholesale-volume decrease, but leaves the other regional distinctions as unexplained line references. It omits the immediately adjacent global wholesale attribution to both price increases and higher volumes (original MD&A / Net revenues by channel / Wholesale; derived line 1147). Europe Q3 wholesale growth from higher volume (1139) and Asia Q3 store performance plus ecommerce demand (1144) deserve equally explicit treatment. This is an omission, not a finding that the gate positively asserts a global volume decline. Its price-only wholesale wording must stay scoped to identified regional/brand components, rather than be read as the global explanation.

Required correction: preserve those favorable original-source attributions in the parent disposition or a new revision, alongside the Americas evidence. They prevent an unduly adverse demand narrative but do not identify the incremental return on promotional spending. No new forecast or earlier calculation challenge is required.

**RD-02 — Low, nonblocking for the retained park: make the comparable-sales brand boundary explicit.** The release defines the metric for the existing Levi's-brand eligible company-owned store base and owned digital channels, with constant-currency and portfolio/calendar rules. Aggregate reported ecommerce growth and channel weights are not thereby the same population. In addition to matching quarter and eligibility, a weighted growth decomposition would need the correct prior-period eligible revenue weights and matched brand/currency scope. The YTD current/prior percentages supply neither a quarter-specific eligible-store weight nor the required ecommerce comparable-growth series.

Required correction: put that explicit definition into the parent evidence disposition or new revision. The original no-negative-store-comp inference remains correct. This is precision needed for future reuse, not a reason to reopen a closed diagnostic.

## Tax, shares, periods and add-back logic

The gate's proposed earnings-bridge discipline is sound within scope: use continuing-operation net income, appropriate item-specific tax effects, matching annual/quarter periods and weighted diluted shares before interpreting an EPS change. The release's approximate 23% FY tax guidance is not the actual tax rate of every quarter or adjustment. Its USD20m tax attribution to the USD84m gross refund/interest benefit is item-specific. No precise new annual earnings bridge was constructed.

The inherited net-refund diagnostic removes a benefit already net of the associated redeployment expense; conceptually it therefore reverses that expense too. Removing the gross gain while retaining actual expenses is a different counterfactual and does not establish recurring EPS. I checked that conceptual distinction against the original footnote; I did not recompute or re-certify the closed 0.37 EPS / 12.2% EBIT diagnostics. Reusing the issuer's labeled Q3 EPS effects is not inherently invalid solely because pretax components occupy different statement lines.

A change in annual EPS-guide midpoints cannot be compared mechanically with one quarter's attributed net benefit to prove a recurring downgrade. The annual denominator, remaining-quarter spending and earnings/tax scope need a bridge. Ordinary cash dividends do not mechanically alter share counts; weighted/diluted denominators and adjustment scope can prevent careless quarter-EPS summation. Different quarterly tax rates alone do not: compatible after-tax earnings already reflect those rates. No current ownership-claims schedule is verified here. Unexplained residual earnings change must remain unexplained, not relabeled persistent.

## Judgment, contrary interpretations and limits

The useful hypothesis is that incremental redeployment supports profitable demand that persists after the intervention. The adverse alternative is support for softer stores or sales obtained through short-lived incentives. Both remain plausible. Recognition timing, channel and geographic mix, store expansion, price and the comparison base can also affect reported growth. The documented favorable Europe/Asia and global-volume evidence argues against indiscriminate global demand weakness, while softer group store traffic argues against treating headline DTC growth as a clean existing-store recovery. Neither side isolates causation or profitability.

A useful next evidentiary step would match period/cohort and brand/channel scope, separate price/volume/traffic/conversion where available, explain the incremental cost versus baseline budget and duration, and connect results to contribution or operating profit. It need not demand proprietary CAC/LTV, an exact controlled experiment, or exact continuing CFO as universal prerequisites. Information that cannot sufficiently distinguish the hypotheses should limit the particular quantitative conclusion. Sustained profitable price increases can themselves be economically valuable; a lack of unit acceleration alone is not a general rejection rule.

The 10-Q's group statements are management attributions, not independent customer-demand measurements. An anticipated Q4 acceleration is still forward guidance. A later Q4 outcome would require comparable period, seasonality, cost and base checks, and would not alone establish persistence.
