Freeze a pre-event expectations snapshot before release: provider/source version, retrieval and actual estimate timestamps where available, period, currency, adjustment/split basis, coverage/dispersion, management guide, known news and explicit model assumptions. 2. Capture actual future release and interview timestamps independently, including retrieval/availability delay and material intervening news. Freeze a causal recurring-earnings/cash revision with contrary interpretation. A transcript later published is not proof that all its information was available at release. An event date is not an economic timing mechanism by itself.
3. Obtain properly timestamped split-consistent research prices/volume: pre-event regular close, release-window/after-hours observations, next-session open and subsequent hours/days, matched market/sector reference and news chronology. Price movement does not prove causation or economic mispricing.

## How the accepted framework can inform a later dossier

Use it to identify whether revised demand, operating conversion, content productivity, tax or funding assumptions could durably change per-claim future owner cash, including the uncovered-cost downside and terminal dependence. It supplies an inspectable causal scaffold and sensitivity context, not a stock-price reaction function. Repeating the ten-year arithmetic is unnecessary for this gate. No current quote is compared with its printed components.

A later total-equity valuation minimally needs a **consistent actual valuation-date corporate roll-forward**: (a) unrestricted/restricted cash, liquid investments, required operating/content liquidity and genuinely surplus cash; (b) principal/carrying debt differences, actual July issuance proceeds/expenses, subsequent repayment/new financing, coupons/FX and debt-service dates; (c) actual common shares and options/unvested/convertible claims, exercise proceeds/settlement and overlap with compensation costs, including subsequent issuance/buybacks; (d) intervening dividends/buybacks/acquisitions, tax/deposit obligations and nonoperating claims/assets that affect available value; and (e) a coherent valuation-date-to-January cash/stub and distribution bridge. Public interim filings and subsequent releases can supply dated observations with qualified rolls; daily perfect disclosure is not demanded. The current component already deducts debt principal/interest through the cash paths. Do **not** subtract net debt again or blindly add all June cash. Apply actual time discounting, stub economics and distributions consistently; merely discounting a January component to October does not supply the missing bridge. This gate defines missing evidence, not a new accepted valuation treatment.

## Blockers, contrary findings and review boundary

## Analytical challenge

**Forecast cases.** Historical aggregate incremental operating conversion is an imperfect forecast guide, but the use here is transparent. An explicit decade lets the higher-growth path decelerate before maturity. These are defensible conditional scenario constructions, not independently measured future marginal economics. Revenue stagnation is not the worst imaginable downside; permanent loss of relevance, sustained contraction or substantially higher content costs can produce worse outcomes. The paper expressly disclaims certified bounds, so that limitation does not prevent retaining the framework.

**Content and other reinvestment.** The arithmetic funds the greater of the fading initial cash-amortisation excess and the assumed net asset-growth requirement. Asset stocks reconcile each year; terminal net content and noncontent asset growth is funded. This closes the free-growth problem. It does not prove the assumed amortisation schedule or content productivity: cash additions, licensed-content liabilities, in-production assets, release timing, tax incentives and FX are simplified into a normalised asset roll. The annual filing describes accelerated amortisation and over 90% amortisation on average within four years after availability; the model is an aggregate cash scenario, not a title-cohort accounting reconstruction.
