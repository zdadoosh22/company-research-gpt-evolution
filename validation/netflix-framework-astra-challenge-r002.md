## Analytical challenge

**Forecast cases.** Historical aggregate incremental operating conversion is an imperfect forecast guide, but the use here is transparent. The initial downside margin reset and cash-cost stresses produce a different economic path; central/upside use different conversion and growth fades rather than cosmetic price ranges. An explicit decade lets the higher-growth path decelerate before maturity. These are defensible conditional scenario constructions, not independently measured future marginal economics. Revenue stagnation is not the worst imaginable downside; permanent loss of relevance, sustained contraction or substantially higher content costs can produce worse outcomes. The paper expressly disclaims certified bounds, so that limitation does not prevent retaining the framework.

**Content and other reinvestment.** The arithmetic funds the greater of the fading initial cash-amortisation excess and the assumed net asset-growth requirement. Asset stocks reconcile each year; terminal net content and noncontent asset growth is funded. This closes the free-growth problem. It does not prove the assumed amortisation schedule or content productivity: cash additions, licensed-content liabilities, in-production assets, release timing, tax incentives and FX are simplified into a normalised asset roll. The annual filing describes accelerated amortisation and over 90% amortisation on average within four years after availability; the model is an aggregate cash scenario, not a title-cohort accounting reconstruction.

The upside's half-intensity growth investment is its clearest discretionary productivity assumption. I tested full intensity instead while keeping the same upside revenue, operating conversion, rate and $4bn uncovered schedule. The per-gross-claim component decreases from **$52.27315 to $49.63860**, about **5.0%**. This is an alternative assumption sensitivity, not correction of a factual error. It supports retaining the explicit productivity caveat. A change in operating conversion at the same time could be more consequential.

**Taxes, compensation and cash reconciliation.** The prospective 20/25/30% tax assumptions are independently stated stresses on the consistent core earnings population; none purports to allocate the receipt tax or set a legal maximum. GAAP operating income retains compensation; no future SBC cash addback or automatic buyback-driven share reduction appears. The existing gross-award denominator and exclusion of exercise proceeds are explicitly conservative dated claims conditions; possible overlap with existing unvested compensation is acknowledged. The historical $801.336m cash-bridge difference is fully decomposed and is not inserted into forecasts as recurring cash. Working-capital intensity and net-capital-expenditure fades are assumptions tied to actual inputs, not proved normalised requirements. Zero acquisition benefits in the central/upside cases and the downside recurring acquisition stress are consistently distinguished.

**Commitments.** The $0/$2/$4/$6bn uncovered schedules are actually deducted at specified dates, rather than merely compared with annual cash. The final 2029 payment receives midyear discounting; the schedule falls mostly beyond June 2027 and within the disclosed three-year window. Known liabilities and known commitments are not subtracted again. Withholding tax benefits, replacement savings and extra asset value from the stress is conservative. The $6bn case usefully demonstrates that the management estimate is not a hard upper bound. The ordinary planned content cash remains a conditional coverage assumption; this model does not certify every future contract.

**Debt, dates, claims and liquidity.** I checked the June debt-note principal tranches and maturity months and the July new issue against the source texts. The model rolls the November repayment/new issue into an explicitly pro forma January principal, deducts the scheduled principal once and separately handles the post-2036 residual debt/interest. There is no free borrowing receipt or second subtraction of the entire debt balance. The 6% average-principal interest convention and approximate 2054 tail are clearly disclosed; they are not precise cash coupons, and floating-rate hedges/FX can differ. At this scope, those approximations do not defeat the construction.

The January 2027 reference avoids treating already elapsed FY2026 cash as a future flow. Opening cash and claims are conditions based on historical observations, not actual January balances. Omission of opening surplus-cash value makes the outputs cash-franchise components, not complete equity values. Annual reserve adequacy under the stated opening condition is demonstrated; current corporate liquidity and intrayear availability are not. Discounted negative FCFE remains an economic funding cost even when the assumed reserve covers it. No actual dividend policy or payable distribution is inferred.

**Rates and maturity.** The rate follows the disclosed adjusted-dollar reference and ERP plus a separately labelled 1.0–1.5 risk-multiplier range. The NYU industry numbers are unlevered business-risk references, not Netflix equity betas; the model does not claim otherwise. A constant judgemental equity required return while debt declines is an approximation, not a measured dynamic cost of capital. It is acceptable for the stated conditional sensitivity, not a certified valuation interval. Terminal growth of zero or 2% is linked to a mature scenario, with actual reinvestment deductions. The Fed target is only a USD nominal reference and cannot guarantee Netflix growth or pricing. Roughly half the component is terminal value, so durability and mature cash conversion deserve substantial weight in interpretation.

**Cross-check and expectations.** Applying historical FCF-minus-SBC/GAAP-net-income conversion to future cash-tax earnings is a useful rough diagnostic with acknowledged definition differences. It is not fully independent underwriting and does not validate content productivity. The same-input terminal earnings expression is only an identity, correctly labelled. Disagreements are explained rather than averaged. The frozen management/provider baselines, unmatched EPS definitions, possible no-repricing/compression and manual response delays remain separated from the long-run cash components. This supports later criticism/planning, not an actual event-price-response conclusion or an instruction to monitor automatically.
